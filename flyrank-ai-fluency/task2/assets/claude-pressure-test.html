Pressure-test the sitemap

A skeptical founder response, then the one change I made.

YOU
Prompt

Act as a skeptical seed-stage SaaS founder. I have a flaky background-job pipeline and I might hire someone for a short reliability sprint.

Claim: I design idempotent, crash-safe backend workflows in Node.js.
Person: a seed-stage SaaS founder or engineering lead with a flaky queue, webhook, or retry path and no platform engineer.
Action: book a 20-minute reliability review.

Sitemap v1: Home (claim, three case cards, CTA) · Work index · Social Media Studio case (26 tests) · Supabase Auth API case (16 tests) · Polite Books to Scrape case (60 records, 11 tests, broken URL) · About · Contact.

Pressure-test it: 1) Which page is doing no work toward the action? 2) Where would you stop believing me? 3) What proof would you need before booking? 4) What should I cut, reorder, or rewrite? Be blunt. End with the single smallest change with the highest impact.

CL
Tutor output

1 · Which page is doing no work?

The Work index. Home already has the three cards. The index repeats the same three links and adds a click between me and the proof. Cut it. Six pages is already generous for one action.

2 · Where would I stop believing you?

At the Home page if it opens with a portrait and a paragraph about curiosity. I do not need a personality; I need to know whether you have seen my failure. Put the retry problem in the first screen. “One post after a timeout” is the line that makes me keep reading.

The About page is the second risk. If it turns into a CV with every tool you have touched, I will assume the cases are decoration. Keep it to why you care about failure, three proof links, and the CTA.

3 · What proof would I need before booking?

I want to see that you can tell the difference between “it retries” and “it does not duplicate”. Show me a test where the platform accepted the write and the response was lost, then show the retry producing one post. Show me a stale lease after a worker crash. A screenshot of a green test count is not enough; name the failure.

I also want one small sign that you can work with a real system: status codes, logs, or a transcript. The auth case can do that if you keep the status table short.

4 · What should I cut, reorder, or rewrite?

Cut the Work index. Keep Home, the three cases, About, Contact.

Reorder Home: claim → proof strip → three cases → about → CTA. The proof strip should be three lines, not three paragraphs: 16 auth tests, one post after a timeout, 60 records when a URL breaks. Make each number link to the case.

On the case pages, put the failure above the technology. I do not care that it is Node.js until I know it survives a duplicate.

Rewrite the CTA so it asks for one small thing: “Bring one failing retry path to a 20-minute review.” That is easier to say yes to than “book a call”.

Smallest high-impact change: cut /work and add a three-line proof strip above the case cards on Home.
Decision recorded: cut /work, add a three-line proof strip above the case cards, and keep the sitemap at six pages. The next test is the Home page order, not more pages.
